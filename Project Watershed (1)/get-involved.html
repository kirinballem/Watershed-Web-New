Get Involved

This is the watershed moment for America's water loss crisis.

We're making sure no utility can hide from it.

Project Watershed chapters are forming now. Every chapter starts the same way: adopt one utility, run one grade, make one presentation.

The Model

What a chapter does

Research

Adopt your local utility. Pull its publicly available water audit data. Run its grade using the Project Watershed methodology. Understand what your community is actually paying for.

Advocate

Share your findings publicly, on social media, on your chapter's page on our website, and with local news outlets. We're building the infrastructure to make this automatic: every chapter will get a login, upload their utility's data, and the site will turn it into public charts and visuals.

Create Change

Put your findings in front of the people who can act on them: city council, your utility board, and local policymakers. A grade only matters if it reaches someone with the power to fix what it shows.

Chapter Requirements

What joining actually requires

Regular

  • Monthly chapter meetings to review local utility data and coordinate advocacy actions
  • Minimum of 5 members per chapter
  • Posts to their own chapter social media, press outlet
  • Contribution to the national report card data collection each spring
  • Attend Watershed Summit (More Details Coming Soon)

Additionally

  • School awareness campaigns around the annual report card release
  • Community events and public education efforts
  • Outreach to your local utility directly

The Payoff

What members get

For students

  • Real research experience working with public government data
  • Direct civic advocacy experience presenting findings to elected officials and government bodies
  • Membership in a growing national student network
  • A concrete, documented, citable project for college applications, not a club you joined, a movement you helped build
  • The chance to create measurable, verifiable change in your own community before you graduate high school

For schools

  • A structured, replicable civic engagement program tied to real policy outcomes
  • Student work product that reflects genuine research and advocacy skills
  • Affiliation with a national student-led nonprofit with academic credibility and press coverage
  • A model that aligns with civic education, environmental studies, and public policy curricula
